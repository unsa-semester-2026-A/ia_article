\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The completed final-artifact analysis defines the interpretation of the study. The homogeneous final evaluator ZIPs show that all six conditions obtain 0.000 Macro AP-rIoU. The appropriate interpretation is therefore not that one augmentation strategy outperformed another, but that the current training and evaluation pipeline exposes a systematic failure at the final matching stage. This is still a meaningful research result because it identifies where the ablation evidence is valid and where stronger claims would be premature.

In the context of traffic-surveillance literature, the results reinforce a distinction that is sometimes hidden by deployment-oriented studies. YOLO-based traffic systems are often valued because they can support detection, counting, and tracking at useful speed~\cite{Gomaa2022,Kumar2023,BinZuraimi2021}. The present study also obtains real-time or near-real-time throughput for trained conditions, ranging from 83.8 to 98.9 FPS. However, speed is not equivalent to task validity. Under a class-aware OBB metric with motion filtering, a fast detector that produces many candidates can still obtain zero AP if those candidates do not match the official class and rotated geometry. This finding is consistent with traffic-perception surveys that emphasize occlusion, camera viewpoint, and annotation design as practical barriers, not merely inference latency~\cite{Zhou2025TrafficSurveillanceSurvey,Azfar2024ComplexTrafficReview}.

The result is also consistent with the strictness of oriented-object detection literature. OBB methods and rotated losses are developed precisely because orientation, vertex order, center error, and polygon overlap matter in dense scenes~\cite{Ding2022DOTA,Wang2025OBBSurvey,Zand2022OBB,Yang2021KLD}. Oriented R-CNN, ReDet, and S2ANet address these geometric issues inside the model architecture or feature representation~\cite{Xie2021OrientedRCNN,Han2021ReDet,Han2022S2ANet}. In contrast, the present ablation mainly changes the data pipeline while using the same YOLO26s-OBB family. The zero AP at rIoU 0.50 therefore suggests that the next audit should not focus only on whether the synthetic images look realistic. It must also verify the compatibility of class identifiers, OBB coordinate conventions, vertex ordering, confidence handling, and the final evaluator's matching assumptions.

The synthetic-augmentation literature provides a similar caution. Prior work shows that artificial images and object-centric compositions can help in low-data or long-tailed settings when they support the downstream task~\cite{Weber2021ArtificialImagesVehicleDetection,Mumuni2024,HuangW2025}. At the same time, diffusion and controllable-generation surveys warn that visual plausibility does not guarantee label fidelity or evaluation benefit~\cite{Alimisis2025Advances,Benkedadra2024,ZhuH2025}. The SAM copy-paste release in this paper follows a conservative design: it uses real foreground masks, train-only placement slots, deterministic label writing, and validation checks. The final metrics show that this methodological care is necessary but not sufficient. The implemented synthetic delta did not produce a verified final AP gain in its current form.

The class-level false-positive distribution also aligns with prior concerns about long-tailed and small-object recognition. Studies on motorcycle detection and small-object detection note that compact targets, crowded context, and class imbalance can weaken recognition~\cite{Espinosa2020,Cheng2023SmallObjectSurvey,Nikouei2025SmallObjectSurvey}. In this ablation, Motocicleta remains a major source of unmatched detections for the trained conditions, and Auto dominates the overall candidate volume because of its frequency. Improvement C reduces Motocicleta false positives relative to Improvement A and B, but the absence of true positives shows that reducing candidate volume alone does not solve the class-and-geometry matching problem.

The impact of the study is therefore methodological and diagnostic. It contributes a reproducible train-only augmentation workflow and a final evaluation protocol that prevents unsupported performance claims. This matters because internal training metrics can look strong, as shown by Improvement B's mAP50 of 0.929, while the final task metric remains zero. The study demonstrates why ablation papers should keep validation data unchanged, separate training diagnostics from final metrics, and preserve evaluator artifacts for audit.

The main implication is that the current system is not ready to claim an improvement in oriented vehicle detection accuracy. Its value is that it narrows the next engineering and research problem. Before adding more synthetic data or changing architectures, the pipeline should be audited on a small, visually inspected validation subset with filter on/off scoring. If that audit confirms that class IDs, OBB geometry, and evaluator inputs are correct, then the zero AP would indicate a genuine modeling failure. If it finds a conversion or compatibility error, the current artifact set becomes evidence for improving the evaluation chain rather than evidence against the augmentation idea itself.

Finally, the shift away from IC-Light remains justified for this article. IC-Light was useful as exploratory visual work, but the production contribution is segmentation-assisted copy-paste because it preserves a direct relation between real vehicle pixels and explicit OBB labels. The final results do not prove that copy-paste improves Macro AP-rIoU, but they do support the article's more defensible contribution: a controlled augmentation and evaluation framework that makes negative or unexpected outcomes visible.
