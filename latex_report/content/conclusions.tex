\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This work presented a data-centric ablation study for \textit{Segmentation-Assisted Synthetic Augmentation for Oriented Vehicle Detection in Peruvian Traffic Surveillance}. The study audited the SMART traffic annotations, converted parametric vehicle boxes to YOLO-OBB labels, preserved a clip-level validation split, generated a train-only SAM copy-paste augmentation release, trained YOLO26s-OBB conditions, and evaluated final detector artifacts with Macro AP-rIoU after homography-based motion filtering. The implemented augmentation release contains 1,501 synthetic image-label pairs and 3,391 inserted vehicle instances, with no synthetic validation files.

The main finding is that the completed final evaluator artifacts do not show a detection-performance gain. All six evaluated conditions, Base 0, Base 1, Base 2, Improvement A, Improvement B, and Improvement C, report 0.000 Macro AP-rIoU, 0.000 AP@0.50, and 0.000 AP@0.80. At rIoU 0.50, every condition has zero true positives and 109,448 false negatives. The models do emit candidate detections, with false-positive counts ranging from 149,092 to 319,557, but none is accepted by the final class-and-rotated-geometry matcher. Improvement C provides the best operational profile among the synthetic/LaMa variants, with 201,344 false positives and 98.9 FPS, but this does not constitute a final AP improvement.

The importance of the research lies in showing why data augmentation for oriented traffic detection must be evaluated with strict and auditable final metrics. Internal training validation can appear promising, as in Improvement B's mAP50 of 0.929, while the final task metric remains zero. This contrast demonstrates that unchanged validation data, official class identifiers, rIoU-based matching, motion filtering, and artifact traceability are necessary to avoid unsupported claims.

The main contribution is therefore methodological and diagnostic. The article provides a reproducible mask-guided copy-paste pipeline, a controlled ablation design, and a final-artifact analysis that identifies the next technical bottleneck: compatibility between model predictions and the final evaluator. As a final observation, the current evidence does not reject the general idea of SAM-assisted copy-paste, but it does show that the implemented version cannot yet be claimed to improve Macro AP-rIoU. The next scientific step is a focused evaluation audit, followed by repeated ablations and refined rare-class augmentation once the final matching chain is verified.
