\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This work presented a data-centric ablation study for oriented vehicle detection in Peruvian traffic surveillance imagery. The workflow audits the dataset, converts annotations to YOLO-OBB format, builds a train-only SAM copy-paste augmentation release, and evaluates detections with Macro AP-rIoU after homography-based motion filtering. The implemented augmentation set contains 1,501 image-label pairs and 3,391 inserted instances, with validation data left unchanged.

The available final results show that Base 1, the raw-data baseline, is currently the only condition with non-zero final performance: 0.577 Macro AP-rIoU, 0.666 AP@0.50, and 0.411 AP@0.80. Base 0, Base 2, and Improvement A produce candidate detections but obtain zero true positives under the uploaded final evaluator outputs. These results make Base 1 the current reference condition and show that classical online augmentation and LaMa-only training do not provide verified final-metric gains in the available artifacts.

The proposed SAM copy-paste intervention remains scientifically open. Improvement B shows strong internal YOLO validation curves, but its final Macro AP-rIoU package is pending; Improvement C is also pending upload. Therefore, the paper does not claim that synthetic copy-paste has already improved the final detector metric. Its main contribution is a reproducible, label-preserving augmentation pipeline and a controlled ablation framework that can evaluate that claim once the remaining final artifacts are available.
