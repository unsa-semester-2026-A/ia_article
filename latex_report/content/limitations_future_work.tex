\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations_future_work}

The main limitation is that the final evaluator reports zero true positives for every condition, including the raw trained baseline and both synthetic-data conditions. This prevents the study from claiming detector-accuracy improvement. The result is still valid as an artifact-level finding, but it must be interpreted with caution because a universal zero at rIoU 0.50 can indicate either a severe detector failure or a compatibility issue between predictions and the final evaluator.

A second limitation is the absence of a visual audit tied directly to final matching. The artifacts contain raw predictions, filtered predictions, homographies, class-level metrics, and threshold-level metrics, but the current analysis does not yet include frame-by-frame overlays of matched and unmatched predictions. Without that manual inspection, the paper cannot determine whether the zero AP comes from class remapping, OBB vertex order, coordinate convention, excessive filtering, confidence handling, checkpoint selection, or genuine visual-model failure.

The study also uses one validation split and one seed per F1 condition. This supports a controlled ablation, but it does not estimate training variance. In addition, the experiments are evaluated on one traffic-imagery dataset and one final evaluator implementation. The results therefore should not be generalized to all traffic OBB tasks or all copy-paste augmentation pipelines without further validation.

Future work should first perform a compatibility audit of the final evaluator. A small subset of validation frames should be scored with visual overlays, known hand-checked predictions, and motion filtering disabled and enabled. This subset should verify that official class identifiers, YOLO-OBB vertex order, pixel-coordinate conversion, angle conventions, and frame names are preserved from prediction export to final scoring. This is the highest-priority continuation because it determines whether the current zero AP reflects the models or the evaluation interface.

After that audit, the next line of work should repeat the ablation under multiple seeds and export confidence intervals or bootstrap variability for Macro AP-rIoU, AP@0.50, and class-wise AP. The same final evaluator schema should be preserved for every condition so that future studies can compare results without artifact heterogeneity.

Further work should also refine the augmentation strategy itself. The SAM copy-paste delta can be evaluated with per-class quotas, context-aware placement, stronger rejection of visually implausible insertions, and class-specific sampling for Motocicleta, Articulado, Combi, Microbus, Minibus, Omnibus, and Mototaxi. Since Improvement C reduced false positives and improved throughput but did not recover true positives, future experiments should separate the effects of LaMa correction, foreground-mask quality, target-slot selection, and label geometry.

Finally, future versions should compare the data-centric approach with complementary model-level strategies, such as class-balanced losses, rare-class oversampling, small-object resolution changes, OBB-specific loss tuning, and architectures designed for rotated detection. The long-term goal is not only to generate synthetic training examples, but to show that those examples improve class-aware rotated localization under an unchanged and auditable validation protocol.
