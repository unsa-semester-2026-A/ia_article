\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

The literature connected to this study spans traffic surveillance systems, oriented localization, class imbalance, and synthetic augmentation. Traffic-perception surveys describe the main operational obstacles: lighting variation, occlusion, congestion, camera placement, annotation cost, and heterogeneous vehicle fleets~\cite{Zhou2025TrafficSurveillanceSurvey,Azfar2024ComplexTrafficReview}. YOLO-based traffic systems show why single-stage detectors remain attractive in this domain. Gomaa et al.~\cite{Gomaa2022} combine intermittent YOLOv2 detections with KLT optical flow for vehicle counting, Kumar et al.~\cite{Kumar2023} pair YOLOv5 with DeepSORT for real-time traffic management, and Bin Zuraimi and Kamaru Zaman~\cite{BinZuraimi2021} compare YOLOv3/v4 variants within a detection-tracking pipeline. These studies establish the practical value of fast detectors, although their main outputs are horizontal boxes, tracks, or counts rather than class-aware OBB augmentation.

Work on deployment adds a complementary constraint: a traffic detector must be useful under real hardware and camera-network limitations. Balamuralidhar et al.~\cite{Balamuralidhar2021} present a UAV monitoring system that shares visual features across detection, tracking, speed estimation, and segmentation on embedded hardware. Alas et al.~\cite{Alas2026} consider multi-camera urban tracking, where association across CCTV streams must scale without assigning a high-end GPU to every camera. This line of work is important for municipal use cases, but it addresses tracking, counting, or re-identification more directly than the per-frame rotated-box augmentation problem considered here.

Another relevant thread is domain generalization across cameras. Koga et al.~\cite{Koga2021VehicleDomainAdaptation} study vehicle detector adaptation between different source and target domains using adversarial prediction alignment. Vora et al.~\cite{Vora2023GeneralizationMultiview} examine generalization in multi-view pedestrian detection under changes in camera placement and scene. Although these works do not target Lima traffic imagery, they motivate the same methodological concern: if all training evidence comes from a small set of observed frames, a detector may learn background or viewpoint regularities that are not robust. Augmentation is therefore useful only if it increases diversity without corrupting the annotation geometry.

The use of OBBs responds to geometric limitations of horizontal boxes. Remote-sensing benchmarks and surveys have emphasized this point for dense scenes, arbitrary headings, and small targets~\cite{Ding2022DOTA,Wang2025OBBSurvey,Zand2022OBB}. Several detector designs then try to make the model itself orientation-aware: Oriented R-CNN aligns proposals with rotated objects~\cite{Xie2021OrientedRCNN}, ReDet introduces rotation-equivariant representation learning~\cite{Han2021ReDet}, and S2ANet aligns features for oriented detection~\cite{Han2022S2ANet}. Loss formulations based on Gaussian or Mahalanobis distances further illustrate why angular error is not a minor detail in high-precision rotated localization~\cite{Yang2021KLD,Wen2022MahalanobisRotatedDetection}. For vehicle analysis, Ahmed et al.~\cite{Ahmed2025} show that OBB and segmentation-assisted representations can reduce measurement error relative to axis-aligned boxes.

The YOLO family remains relevant because traffic applications often need a compromise between accuracy, latency, and deployability. YOLOv10 strengthens the case for end-to-end single-stage detection by reducing reliance on conventional post-processing~\cite{Wang2024YOLOv10}. The present study uses the Ultralytics YOLO26s-OBB implementation, consistent with the YOLO26 goal of covering detection, segmentation, pose, classification, and oriented detection within a unified real-time framework~\cite{Jocher2026}. OBB-specific work such as task-wise sampling convolutions also shows that classification and localization features can become inconsistent if orientation is not handled carefully~\cite{Huang2025TSConv}. Thus, the open issue is not whether OBB detectors exist, but how to prepare and evaluate data for a long-tailed urban traffic taxonomy.

Long-tailed class frequencies and small object scale are particularly important in heterogeneous traffic. Espinosa et al.~\cite{Espinosa2020} review motorcycle detection and highlight the need for focused treatment of vulnerable or minority road-user categories. For oriented aerial vehicles, Mo and Yan~\cite{Mo2020} mitigate class imbalance by increasing minority-class exposure, preserving finer feature information, and adding a category-separation objective. More broadly, small-object surveys explain why compact targets can disappear in deep feature hierarchies or become ambiguous in dense context~\cite{Cheng2023SmallObjectSurvey,Nikouei2025SmallObjectSurvey}. These studies justify data-level balancing, but they do not define a train-only procedure for adding rare traffic vehicles while preserving OBB labels.

Synthetic augmentation is one way to address limited diversity, but the literature separates usefulness from visual plausibility. Weber et al.~\cite{Weber2021ArtificialImagesVehicleDetection} report that artificial aerial vehicle images can help in low-data regimes when combined with real samples. Mumuni et al.~\cite{Mumuni2024} survey augmentation families and note that real-plus-synthetic training can be effective when the synthetic examples support the downstream task. Diffusion-based augmentation adds semantic flexibility, while Alimisis et al.~\cite{Alimisis2025Advances} identify bias and computational cost as continuing concerns. CIA includes an optional quality-assessment module~\cite{Benkedadra2024}, whereas ReCon addresses spatial--semantic alignment~\cite{ZhuH2025}. These concerns are central for OBB detection because a visually convincing vehicle is still harmful if its boundary or rotated label no longer matches the pixels.

Segmentation-assisted composition offers a more controlled position between full-frame generation and ordinary geometric augmentation. Rahat et al.~\cite{Rahat2024} combine off-the-shelf segmentation with generative background diversification to preserve foreground subjects. Huang et al.~\cite{HuangW2025} analyze object-centric synthetic compositions and point out that simple copy-paste may introduce edge artifacts or context mismatch. The method implemented in this paper is deliberately conservative relative to those generative approaches. It extracts real vehicles with SAM masks, places them only in train-split slots, preserves background pixels outside the mask, and writes OBB labels directly from target geometry.

Table~\ref{tab:related_work_summary} summarizes the connection between these research lines and the present contribution. Taken together, prior studies provide evidence for real-time traffic detection, deployment constraints, OBB geometry, class imbalance, and synthetic data. Against this literature, the study examines a specific methodological combination: train-only, segmentation-assisted copy-paste for a long-tailed traffic OBB dataset, unchanged validation data, and rotated-geometry evaluation.
