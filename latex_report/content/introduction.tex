\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Traffic video is a practical source of evidence for urban mobility studies because many cities already operate fixed cameras. The difficulty is that the visual signal is not standardized. A vehicle may be partly occluded, compressed into a small image region, affected by shadows, or seen from a camera angle that is absent from common training data~\cite{Zou2023ObjectDetectionSurvey,Zhou2025TrafficSurveillanceSurvey,Azfar2024ComplexTrafficReview}. For an automated traffic workflow, these errors are not cosmetic: they can propagate to counting, lane-use analysis, trajectory reconstruction, and later planning decisions.

The Lima clips used here make the problem more specific. The camera frames contain dense and heterogeneous traffic, including frequent cars and less frequent classes such as buses, mototaxis, motorcycles, and articulated vehicles. A detector trained only on the available frames can learn camera-specific context, while minority classes contribute fewer examples to the loss. Cross-camera and multi-view studies report the same type of weakness when viewpoint, illumination, scale, or scene layout changes between training and deployment~\cite{Koga2021VehicleDomainAdaptation,Vora2023GeneralizationMultiview}. Data preparation is evaluated as part of the detector design, not as a secondary preprocessing detail.

Localization is also stricter than in a horizontal-box traffic detector. In elevated traffic views, vehicles often appear diagonally, turn across lanes, or lie close to neighboring objects. Oriented bounding boxes (OBBs) follow the vehicle footprint more tightly than axis-aligned boxes, but they also expose errors in angle, vertex order, scale, and center position~\cite{Wang2025OBBSurvey,Zand2022OBB,Ahmed2025}. Scoring is based on rotated IoU rather than horizontal overlap.

Class imbalance becomes more severe under this OBB protocol. Frequent cars can dominate the training signal, while small or rare categories may need more visual evidence to learn both class identity and rotated geometry. Prior work on motorcycles, small objects, and oriented aerial vehicles links this failure mode to limited pixels, clutter, and low class support~\cite{Espinosa2020,Cheng2023SmallObjectSurvey,Nikouei2025SmallObjectSurvey,Mo2020}. A class-balanced metric is consequently needed; otherwise, a high score for Auto could hide poor behavior on Motocicleta, Combi, or Articulado.

Synthetic augmentation is one possible response, but it is risky for OBB detection. Full-frame synthesis can add variation, yet it may also shift object boundaries or create pixels that no longer match the rotated label. The approach tested here is narrower: real vehicles are extracted with segmentation masks, pasted only into selected training slots, and assigned explicit OBB labels. This design follows object-centric augmentation work while avoiding synthetic validation data~\cite{Rahat2024,Mumuni2024,HuangW2025}. IC-Light relighting~\cite{Zhang2025ICLight} was explored during development, but the final production path prioritizes deterministic label preservation.

The article treats that design as an ablation, not as an assumed improvement. The pipeline audits the dataset, converts annotations to YOLO-OBB format, extracts foregrounds with the Segment Anything Model (SAM)~\cite{Kirillov2023SAM}, writes train-only copy-paste samples, trains YOLO26s-OBB conditions, and evaluates final artifacts with Macro AP-rIoU. The updated results are conservative: Base 1, the raw-data baseline, is the strongest final detector; Base 2 is nearly tied and has the best AP@0.80; Improvement C improves speed and false-positive behavior among the synthetic/LaMa variants but does not surpass Base 1 in the main score.

The paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:theoretical_background} defines the detection, OBB, imbalance, and Macro AP-rIoU concepts used in the study. Section~\ref{sec:related_work} positions the work against traffic detection, oriented localization, and synthetic augmentation literature. Section~\ref{sec:material_methods} describes the dataset, augmentation workflow, training conditions, motion filter, and evaluation protocol. Section~\ref{sec:results_discussion} reports the final artifacts and their interpretation, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} closes with the main findings and future work.
