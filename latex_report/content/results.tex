\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results_discussion}

\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The final comparison uses the updated evaluator ZIP artifacts for Base 0, Base 1, Base 2, Improvement A, Improvement B, and Improvement C. Each package stores summary metrics, class metrics, threshold metrics, hardware diagnostics, raw and filtered predictions, homographies, coordinate-correction metadata, and manifest metadata. All values reported below come from those packages, so training logs and local smoke tests are not mixed with final validation metrics.

The evaluator uses 10,873 validation frames and 109,448 ground-truth vehicle instances. Every condition passes through the same class remapping, coordinate correction, homography-based motion filter, and Macro AP-rIoU code. The metric averages AP over the nine official classes and the seven rIoU thresholds from 0.50 to 0.80. Figure~\ref{fig:final_evaluation_flow} summarizes the flow used for the comparison.

\subsubsection{Dataset and Augmentation Artifacts}
\label{sec:dataset_characterization_results}

The preprocessing audit gives the scale of the experiment: 54,262 source frames, 1,088 clips, and 601,934 oriented-box annotations. The validation side of the final evaluator contains 10,873 frames and 109,448 ground-truth instances. Detector quality is measured on that fixed validation partition, not on synthetic, resampled, or augmented validation images.

The train-only augmentation release, named SAM CP production v1, contains 1,501 synthetic images and 1,501 matching YOLO-OBB label files, with 3,391 inserted vehicle instances. No synthetic validation files are generated. This distinction is central to the ablation: synthetic examples are allowed to influence training, but the validation frames remain raw and unchanged. Table~\ref{tab:augmentation_release_summary} summarizes the generated augmentation set.

The validation checks cover image-label stem mismatches, empty deltas, malformed YOLO-OBB labels, out-of-range coordinates, synthetic files that shadow real frames, and synthetic backgrounds taken from outside the train split. These checks make the augmentation release traceable, but they are not performance evidence. The performance claim must come from the final evaluator.

\subsubsection{Final Detection Metrics}
\label{sec:final_macro_ap_results}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation_final_metrics} and Figure~\ref{fig:ablation_macro_metrics} contain the main result. Task-specific training changes the scale of the metric: Base 0 reaches only 0.078 Macro AP-rIoU, whereas the trained conditions lie between 0.652 and 0.692. Base 1 is the best final detector, with 0.692 Macro AP-rIoU, 0.766 AP@0.50, and 0.537 AP@0.80. Base 2 is close in the main score (0.689) and slightly stronger at the strictest reported threshold, AP@0.80, where it reaches 0.542.

The synthetic and LaMa-related conditions do not overtake the raw-data baseline. Improvement C is the strongest of those variants, with 0.668 Macro AP-rIoU and 0.752 AP@0.50, but it remains 0.023 below Base 1 in the main score. Improvement A and Improvement B are lower, at 0.652 and 0.655. In this artifact set, the implemented SAM copy-paste delta cannot be reported as an accuracy gain over the raw baseline.

The count-level analysis explains why Base 1 is ranked first even though Base 2 has slightly higher recall. Base 2 recovers 86,916 true positives at rIoU 0.50, compared with 86,098 for Base 1, but it also produces 165,974 false positives, more than twice the 62,994 false positives of Base 1. As a result, Base 1 has the highest precision (0.578) and F1 (0.666). Improvement C has a useful intermediate profile: its recall is close to Base 1 and Base 2, and its false-positive count is much lower than Base 2, Improvement A, and Improvement B, but it still does not reach the precision or Macro AP-rIoU of Base 1.

\subsubsection{Threshold Sensitivity}
\label{sec:threshold_sensitivity_results}

Table~\ref{tab:threshold_metrics} shows the mean AP across classes for each rIoU threshold. The same ranking is visible across most thresholds. Base 1 is the best condition from rIoU 0.50 to 0.75, while Base 2 becomes the best at rIoU 0.80. The pattern indicates a small strict-localization benefit for the stronger classical augmentation profile in Base 2, but the gain at the highest threshold is not large enough to compensate for its higher false-positive load in the averaged Macro AP-rIoU score.

Small classes can show high AP when the few examples are consistently recovered, but they should be discussed together with their support.

\label{sec:operational_diagnostics_results}

The motion-filter and runtime diagnostics are shown in Table~\ref{tab:motion_filter_diagnostics}. \subsubsection{False-Positive Distribution}
\label{sec:false_positive_distribution_results}

Figure~\ref{fig:class_fp_by_condition} shows the class distribution of false positives at rIoU 0.50. Base 0 is concentrated in Auto and Camion because the zero-shot DOTA mapping only connects pretrained vehicle categories to those two official task classes. For the trained conditions, Auto and Motocicleta dominate the unmatched detections. Base 1 is the cleanest trained model, with 18,861 Auto false positives and 32,632 Motocicleta false positives. Improvement C reduces Motocicleta false positives relative to Base 2, Improvement A, and Improvement B, but still produces more false positives than Base 1.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{latex_report/src/fig/class_fp_by_condition.png}
  \caption{False-positive distribution by class at rIoU 0.50. Values are post-filter false positives; color intensity is log-scaled for readability.}

The class-level false-positive pattern explains why increasing recall alone is insufficient. Base 2 has the highest recall at rIoU 0.50, but its false-positive volume in Auto, Motocicleta, and several minority classes lowers precision. Improvement C reduces this problem relative to Base 2 and the other improvement variants, which suggests a better candidate distribution, but it does not close the gap with Base 1.

\subsubsection{Training Diagnostics Versus Final Metrics}
\label{sec:training_diagnostics_results}

Training-loop metrics remain useful as convergence diagnostics, but they are not the final evidence used for the article. Improvement B illustrates this distinction. Its internal Ultralytics validation curves reach high training-stage scores, including a reported mAP50 near 0.929 in the previous training artifact, while the final evaluator reports 0.655 Macro AP-rIoU and 0.742 AP@0.50 after the paper's official class mapping, motion filter, and rotated-IoU protocol.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.90\textwidth]{latex_report/src/fig/mejora_b_training_curves.png}
  \caption{Internal Ultralytics validation curves for Improvement B. These curves are convergence diagnostics and should not be treated as replacements for the final Macro AP-rIoU comparison.}
  \label{fig:mejora_b_training_curves}

The gap does not make either metric invalid. The training loop measures behavior under the training validation setup; the paper evaluator measures class-balanced rotated-box performance after homography-based motion filtering. Publication claims follow the latter.

\subsubsection{Summary of Key Findings}
\label{sec:key_findings_results}

Table~\ref{tab:key_findings} gives the final takeaways. The ablation produces a clear but negative result for the central augmentation hypothesis: the implemented segmentation-assisted synthetic data does not outperform the raw-data baseline in final Macro AP-rIoU. At the same time, the experiment is informative because it shows where each condition helps: Base 1 provides the best final detector, Base 2 gives the strongest strict-threshold AP, and Improvement C provides the best speed and false-positive profile among the synthetic/LaMa conditions.
