\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section reports the dataset characterization, the generated augmentation set, and the experimental conditions used to evaluate the proposed workflow. The quantitative evidence focuses on the data-generation stage, label consistency, and validation isolation.

\subsection{Dataset Characterization}
\label{sec:dataset_characterization_results}

The preprocessing analysis confirms that the training CSV contains 54,262 frames, 1,088 clips, and 601,934 OBB annotations. The parser validates class identifiers, positive dimensions, frame-to-label correspondence, and the expected long-tailed distribution reported in Table~\ref{tab:dataset_class_distribution}. These checks are necessary because both YOLO training and synthetic augmentation depend on exact image-label pairing. The analysis also supports the decision to split by clip rather than by frame, since adjacent frames share highly similar camera geometry and would otherwise create validation leakage.

\subsection{Augmentation Set}
\label{sec:augmentation_release_results}

The final train-only augmentation set is documented as \texttt{sam\_cp\_production\_v1}. It contains 1,501 synthetic images and 1,501 matching YOLO-OBB label files, with 3,391 inserted vehicle instances. The procedure creates no validation images or labels. The same synthetic delta is used with raw training images in Improvement B and with LaMa-corrected training images in Improvement C. The validation checks cover the main failure modes that could compromise the experiment. They verify image-label stem equality, reject empty deltas, check every generated YOLO-OBB label for nine fields and normalized coordinates, confirm that no synthetic file shadows a real training frame, and ensure that every synthetic background belongs to the train split. The synthetic data are copied as a separate top-level directory, leaving the base dataset and validation files unchanged. These checks establish the integrity of the augmentation set.

\subsection{IC-Light Status}
\label{sec:iclight_status_results}

IC-Light remains present in the codebase as a preliminary visual-comparison path. The available documentation records difficulties with Gradio API exposure, RGBA foreground handling, full-frame background hallucination, and the need for manual review of generated outputs. The production augmentation command does not call the IC-Light client or the relighting renderer. Therefore, IC-Light is treated in this manuscript as an evaluated but discarded alternative, not as part of the final training data pipeline.

\subsection{Training and Evaluation Conditions}
\label{sec:training_evaluation_conditions}

The experimental design defines five YOLO26s-OBB F1 training conditions and one zero-shot baseline. The trained conditions compare raw training pixels, classical online augmentation, LaMa-corrected training pixels, and the SAM copy-paste delta while preserving the same validation split. Each condition uses the same 10,873 validation frames. They also share batch size 16 and a candidate confidence threshold of 0.001. The protocol records raw predictions, filtered predictions, homographies, class-wise metrics, and threshold-wise metrics so that the comparison can be repeated under the same settings. The present analysis defines these conditions but does not attribute accuracy gains to any single augmentation source.

For this reason, the experimental contribution of this section is the validated data-generation process and the controlled definition of the comparison conditions. The results support the feasibility of producing label-consistent synthetic training samples for minority traffic classes, while the effect of those samples on detector accuracy remains a subject for controlled downstream evaluation.
