\begin{abstract}
Traffic-camera imagery offers a practical basis for mobility monitoring, yet rotated vehicle detection is still affected by crowded scenes, oblique viewpoints, shadows, partial visibility, and uneven class frequencies. We investigate a training-only augmentation scheme that pastes segmented vehicle instances while preserving the original validation set. The pipeline reviews the SMART annotations, transforms them into YOLO-OBB format, obtains object masks with the Segment Anything Model, and creates 1,501 additional image--label pairs containing 3,391 pasted vehicles. Six YOLO26s-OBB configurations are then assessed on nine categories using Macro AP-rIoU over seven rotated-IoU thresholds after homography-based motion filtering. The highest overall score is produced by Base 1 (0.6917), with Base 2 close behind (0.6892); Base 2 also records the best AP@0.80 (0.5417). Of the proposed configurations, Improvement C is strongest, attaining 0.6682 Macro AP-rIoU and 98.93 FPS, although neither synthetic-data configuration exceeds the two raw-image baselines. Across the trained models, Motocicleta remains the least accurate category, with mean AP-rIoU below 0.30. These findings indicate that the combined segmentation-assisted procedure offers favorable runtime behavior and improves upon the other proposed variants, but it does not yield the best detection accuracy. The study contributes an auditable OBB-preserving augmentation workflow and a leakage-free protocol for quantifying the effect of synthetic samples.
\end{abstract}
