\begin{abstract}
Fixed cameras in Lima record dense traffic from elevated viewpoints, where vehicles appear with arbitrary headings and several classes have few examples. We evaluate \textit{Ablation Study of Segmentation-Assisted Synthetic Augmentation for Oriented Vehicle Detection in Peruvian Traffic Surveillance}: a train-only copy-paste delta built from real vehicle masks for YOLO26s-OBB. The workflow audits annotations, exports YOLO-OBB labels, extracts foregrounds with Segment Anything Model masks, inserts vehicles only in selected training slots, and compares five trained conditions against a zero-shot reference. Final ZIP artifacts are scored with Macro AP-rIoU over nine vehicle classes and seven rotated-IoU thresholds after homography-based motion filtering. The generated augmentation set contains 1,501 image-label pairs and 3,391 inserted vehicles, but the best final detector is the raw-data baseline. Base 1 reaches 0.692 Macro AP-rIoU, 0.766 AP@0.50, and 0.537 AP@0.80. Base 2 is nearly tied in Macro AP-rIoU (0.689) and is slightly higher at AP@0.80 (0.542). Improvement C is the best synthetic/LaMa variant, with 0.668 Macro AP-rIoU, fewer false positives than the other augmentation variants, and 98.9 FPS. The article contributes a traceable OBB-preserving augmentation and evaluation chain, together with a negative result that is relevant for data-centric traffic detection: the tested synthetic delta changes speed and false-positive behavior, but it does not improve final class-balanced rotated-box AP over raw training data.
\end{abstract}
