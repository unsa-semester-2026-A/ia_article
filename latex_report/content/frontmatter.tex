\begin{abstract}
Urban traffic cameras provide useful evidence for mobility analysis, but vehicle detection in Lima scenes is affected by dense traffic, elevated viewpoints, shadows, occlusions, object orientation, and highly uneven class frequencies. This paper presents \textit{Segmentation-Assisted Synthetic Augmentation for Oriented Vehicle Detection in Peruvian Traffic Surveillance}, a data-centric ablation study designed to add minority-class training examples without changing the validation split. The workflow audits the source annotations, converts parametric rotated boxes to YOLO-OBB labels, extracts real vehicle instances with Segment Anything Model masks, inserts them into train-only safe slots, and evaluates detections with Macro AP-rIoU across nine classes and seven rotated-IoU thresholds after homography-based motion filtering. The implemented augmentation release contains 1,501 synthetic image-label pairs and 3,391 inserted instances, with no synthetic validation files. Among the uploaded final artifacts, the raw-data baseline is the only condition with non-zero final Macro AP-rIoU (0.577), AP@0.50 (0.666), and AP@0.80 (0.411). Zero-shot inference, classical online augmentation, LaMa-only training, and LaMa plus SAM copy-paste produced candidate detections but no true positives under the final evaluator. Improvement B shows strong internal YOLO validation metrics, but its final Macro AP-rIoU package is not available. The contribution is therefore both a reproducible copy-paste augmentation protocol and an evidence-based ablation report that avoids attributing gains not supported by the final evaluator.
\end{abstract}
